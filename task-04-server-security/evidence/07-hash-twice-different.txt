--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. SECURITY EXPLANATION & WHAT THIS PROVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Why the Hashes Differ:
   Argon2id does not hash the password in isolation. It generates a unique 16-byte
   cryptographic salt per hashing operation and evaluates H(password, salt, cost).
   Because the salt is randomized for every invocation, the derived output is
   statistically unique even for identical input strings.

2. Defense Against Precomputed Rainbow Tables:
   An attacker cannot compute a single lookup table of popular passwords (e.g.,
   the top 10 million leaked passwords) and compare it against the database.
   Because every user record possesses a unique 128-bit salt, an attacker would have
   to recompute the entire rainbow table specifically for each individual salt
   ($2^{128}$ possible tables), rendering precomputation attacks economically
   and computationally unviable.

3. Defense Against Cross-Account Password Matching:
   If two parents choose the exact same password (e.g. "SchoolPass2026!"), a fast
   unsalted hash (or single-salted hash) produces identical database entries,
   revealing to any attacker or observer that both accounts share credentials.
   With per-record salts, their stored hashes share zero bytes in common,
   preserving confidentiality across all accounts.

4. Accurate Scope (What This Does and Does Not Prove):
   Observing two differing hashes proves that the implementation automatically
   generates and embeds a distinct salt per invocation, and that the verification
   routine successfully extracts the embedded salt to recompute and verify the
   hash. It does not by itself prove the mathematical entropy of the underlying
   OS random number generator, but confirms adherence to RFC 9106 PHC encoding.
